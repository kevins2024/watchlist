Ranks are each team's poll position entering that week. Records reflect completed games only, tallied up through the week before the one you're viewing — for weeks that haven't happened yet, both simply stay blank rather than showing a placeholder. Before kickoff, the n/100 score is a hype guess built from several small signals that stack rather than one big gate: both teams ranked (strongest), one team ranked (weaker but still real — not nothing, just more ambiguous than two), a hand-picked list of annual rivalry games (⚔️, CFB only for now — plays tighter than records suggest more often than not, though not always: ask to add one we're missing), a conference game (same idea, weaker), broadcast slot, records, and the pre-game point spread when one's posted — never real viewership data, since no free source for that exists. None of these guarantees a good game or a bad one on its own; they just add up. Once a game kicks off, that guess is dropped entirely — a live game shows no rating at all rather than a stale pre-game guess or a live-score guess built on a still half-finished result; the rating comes back the moment it's final. The final score is a completely separate calculation, built almost entirely off the actual result: final margin does the bulk of the work (a tied or OT-caliber finish scores 80, down 2 points per point of margin, floored at 0 by a 40-point margin), plus flat nudges on top rather than more sub-formulas — a followed team winning adds 30, losing subtracts 30, full stop, not scaled by margin; both teams ranked adds up to 20 depending how highly, one team ranked adds a flat 10; and if neither team plays in a power conference (Power 4, plus Notre Dame as the one power-caliber independent — the only other current FBS independent, UConn, doesn't qualify) that's a flat −20 regardless of rank. A confirmed upset adds another 10. None of it stacks to 100 just because your team won — margin still has to bring real jeopardy, or the two teams' pedigree has to bring real prestige, alongside it. Any overtime scores at least 80 on its own, climbing 10 points per extra OT period up to 100 — a game tied after a full regulation is never a laugher, whatever else about it. 🔥 Nail-biter just means the final margin was within 16 — trusts the box score as-is, no attempt to guess whether a close-looking finish was padded by garbage time, since there's no clean way to tell a real 4th-quarter comeback from that. An outright upset always counts, regardless of margin. ♥ follows a team — a strong boost pre-game, and it's the biggest single swing in the final score too, win or lose. 📑 pins a game to your own Watchlist tab, grouped by week, regardless of its score. ☐/☑ marks a game as already watched (dims the row) — separate from the watchlist, so pinning and watched-tracking don't interfere with each other. Every team also has its own small 👁 next to its name — that's the only thing gating its rank, its record, and whether its name highlights as a winner, since a record climbing (or a highlighted name) after you step forward a week gives away who won. It's there for every team, ranked or not, played or not, so the icon itself never hints at anything. The score shows before kickoff and once final (blank while live, on purpose), but the badges behind it (Marquee, Rivalry, Overtime, Close spread, Following, and especially 🔥 Nail-biter, which gives away that a finished game was close) stay hidden until you tap the row-level 👁 instead — that reveal is per-row and never remembered, so every visit starts hidden again. Kickoff order / Watchability sorts whatever week you're looking at either way. Stepping through weeks moves CFB and NFL together — each resolves the same number of weeks from its own current week, so switching tabs mid-browse lands on the correlated point in both seasons. Everything you mark is saved locally and stays put across visits. Data via ESPN's public scoreboard endpoints. Export backup downloads everything you've marked — watchlist, follows, and watched-marks — across every board on this site (CFB, NFL, NHL, Soccer, Int'l Soccer, Rugby) as one JSON file, since it's all stored only in this browser and would otherwise be gone if you cleared site data or switched devices. Import backup restores from a file made this way, replacing whatever's currently saved on this device — it asks for confirmation first since it can't be undone.
