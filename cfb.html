Ranks are each team's poll position entering that week. Records reflect completed games only, tallied up through the week before the one you're viewing — for weeks that haven't happened yet, both simply stay blank rather than showing a placeholder. The n/100 score is a pre-game watchability guess built from rankings (only counts when both teams are ranked — one alone is as likely to be a wipeout as a good game), broadcast slot, records, and the pre-game point spread when one's posted — never real viewership data, since no free source for that exists. Once a game is final, that guess is thrown out entirely and the score becomes a straight read of the result: a final margin within 16 points scores high, scaled by how close it actually got, plus a bump for a followed team or a confirmed upset. Anything wider scores 0 — unless a followed team is the one who won big, which still counts for something but fades the further the margin runs (a 60-point win is boring too). 🔥 Nail-biter just means the final margin was within 16 — trusts the box score as-is, no attempt to guess whether a close-looking finish was padded by garbage time, since there's no clean way to tell a real 4th-quarter comeback from that. An outright upset always counts, regardless of margin. ♥ follows a team — a strong boost pre-game, and it keeps nudging a close finish either way after; a blowout loss doesn't get anything back, only a blowout win does. 📑 pins a game to your own Watchlist tab, grouped by week, regardless of its score. ☐/☑ marks a game as already watched (dims the row) — separate from the watchlist, so pinning and watched-tracking don't interfere with each other. Every team also has its own small 👁 next to its name — that's the only thing gating its rank, its record, and whether its name highlights as a winner, since a record climbing (or a highlighted name) after you step forward a week gives away who won. It's there for every team, ranked or not, played or not, so the icon itself never hints at anything. The score always shows, but the badges behind it (Marquee, Close spread, Following, and especially 🔥 Nail-biter, which gives away that a finished game was close) stay hidden until you tap the row-level 👁 instead — that reveal is per-row and never remembered, so every visit starts hidden again. Kickoff order / Watchability sorts whatever week you're looking at either way. Stepping through weeks moves CFB and NFL together — each resolves the same number of weeks from its own current week, so switching tabs mid-browse lands on the correlated point in both seasons. Everything you mark is saved locally and stays put across visits. Data via ESPN's public scoreboard endpoints.
